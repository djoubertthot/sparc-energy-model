# MOND vs Energy-Mass (E = γ m c²) Model

## Theoretical Foundations

| Aspect | MOND | Energy-Mass Model |
|--------|------|-------------------|
| Origin | Modified Newtonian dynamics below acceleration a0 | Apparent mass from energy via E = γ m c² |
| Key idea | Gravity law changes when a ≪ a0 | Energy (radiation, kinetic, backgrounds) contributes inertia |
| Fundamental relation | μ(a/a0) a = g_N | m_app = E / (γ c²) |

## Parameters
- **MOND**: a0 ≈ 1.2×10^{-10} m/s² (fixed), interpolation function μ
- **Energy model**: a0 (same scale), β ≈ 1.4, ρ0 (free), Y★, age proxy

## Performance on SPARC
- MOND typically achieves lower scatter than the current energy model
- Energy model median RMS ≈ 45 km/s (exploratory)
- Both can produce flat outer rotation curves

## Similarities
- Both use an acceleration scale a0
- Both amplify the effect in low-acceleration regions
- Both reduce the need for particle dark matter in galaxies

## Differences
- MOND modifies the force law; the energy model adds an effective mass density
- MOND is more mature and better calibrated; the energy model is phenomenological
- The energy model attempts a direct link to relativistic mass-energy equivalence

## Limitations of the Energy Model
- Mapping from microscopic energy trapping to the macroscopic formula is incomplete
- Higher scatter than MOND/BTFR on current SPARC fits
- ρ0 remains free

## Conclusion
Both approaches reproduce flat rotation curves by enhancing the gravitational effect at low acceleration. MOND is more predictive today; the energy-mass idea offers a possible physical interpretation rooted in E = γ m c² that remains to be fully developed.
