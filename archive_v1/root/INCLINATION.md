# Inclination and Higher RMS

## Observational Reasons
1. **Deprojection uncertainty**
   - Line-of-sight velocities must be corrected by 1/sin(i).
   - Small errors in inclination near 90° produce large velocity errors.

2. **Beam smearing and resolution**
   - Highly inclined disks suffer more from beam smearing in radio data.
   - Inner rotation curve points are less reliable.

3. **Extinction and stellar light**
   - Optical surface brightness is more affected by dust in edge-on systems.
   - Y★ and baryonic mass estimates become less accurate.

4. **Warps and non-circular motions**
   - Outer disks of highly inclined galaxies often show warps.
   - These introduce additional scatter not captured by a simple circular model.

## Implications for the Model
The higher RMS is largely an observational effect rather than a failure of the energy accumulation idea. Filtering or down-weighting galaxies with i > 75° typically improves the median RMS of the remaining sample.

## Recommendation
When comparing models, report results both for the full sample and for a high-quality subsample (i < 75°).
